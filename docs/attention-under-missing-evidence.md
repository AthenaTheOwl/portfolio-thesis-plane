# A forced attention ranking still needs a coverage label

Method update: October 18, 2026. Proposed experiments below are not reported as executed results.

The five-factor score and forced ATTEND/FREEZE/RETIRE buckets allocate attention. They should not be read as proof that a low-ranked project's underlying thesis failed. A quiet repository can have missing run evidence; a mechanically bottom-ranked project can remain technically sound.

Keep the existing deterministic rubric, but distinguish three reasons in the review: adverse evidence, stale evidence and absent evidence. In a synthetic two-repo comparison, one has a failed held-out test and the other has no current test. Equal totals can justify similar attention budgets while requiring different next actions: fix the former, measure the latter.

The next proposed weekly fixture should include both cases and preserve the forced bucket output. Its human review records why the bucket was assigned and the single piece of evidence that would change the decision. A RETIRE card remains a planning signal; it authorizes no archive or deletion. Refresh only signals whose source or applicability changed, then rerun the mechanical scorer once against the frozen weekly set.
